Until now my projects had working titles: NihonWorld, Generator and Agent Hub. They were only meant to
hold the place while I got on with the actual work, and they did that. But they described the projects
without saying anything about them.

So from today they have real names. All three come from Japanese, because the whole thing exists so I
can learn it.

## Kotomachi Gakuen: the game

*Koto* means word, as in *kotoba*, and *machi* is town: a town made of words. *Gakuen* is a school,
and lots of Japanese schools are called something-Gakuen. So the full name reads a bit like "the Word
Town school". In Japanese it's ことまち学園.

It fits what the game is: a small town made of dioramas, where every place teaches you something. And
it says up front that this is a game for learning, not a game with some Japanese sprinkled on top.

## Kotokiln: the content workshop

This is the old Generator, the tool that makes the game's sentences and exercises. *Koto* again, so you
can tell they're family, plus *kiln*, the oven where pottery gets fired.

The analogy is why I picked it. With pottery, you shape the clay, check it, and fire it, and once it's
fired it doesn't change anymore. Kotokiln works the same way: it proposes content, I review it, and
only then does it get frozen into a package the game can use.

## Ringi: how the AIs work with me

This one took me the longest to pick. In Japanese companies, a *ringi* (稟議) is a proposal that
traditionally goes around the office on paper. Everyone involved reads it and stamps it with their
personal seal, and the person who decides stamps it last. Before that, people talk it through, and
that's *sōdan* (相談), consultation.

That's exactly how Claude, ChatGPT and Codex work with me. They do the *sōdan* first: one proposes, the
others review it and come back with changes. Then it goes around for the stamps, and mine is always the
last one.

## What changes

The site, Jira and Confluence switch to the new names this week, and the old links keep working. The
code takes a bit longer: Kotokiln gets its name when it moves into its own repo, and the game's repo
gets renamed at the next stop of the split, so I'm not renaming things in the middle of moving them.
The older posts keep the old names, because that's what things were called back then.
